# P7.39 — offline MP1 writer provenance

This stage adds no kernel code or hardware access. It does not grant a mailbox lease or authorize commands. P7.36/37 samples are sequential: Response=1 in two samples is not a new firmware response, and equal endpoints do not exclude intervening writes.

## Reproduce

```
python3 tools/navi33_p739_writer_provenance.py --repo . --input /path/to/saved-ioreg.txt --output /path/to/new-report.json
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests/host -p navi33_p739_writer_provenance_test.py
```

The input is the unmodified P7.36/37 RedNaviProbe capture used by P7.38. The output is created exclusively; existing files are not overwritten. Omitting input produces a source-only report and exit 2. Exit 0 means only that P7.38's sample consistency checks and the reviewed P7.34 baseline passed; it never means exclusive ownership. Incomplete, contradictory or duplicate sample fields exit 2. The capture hash identifies the analyzed input; it does not authenticate its origin. The provenance fields separately represent endpoint differences, host attribution, firmware origin, exclusive ownership and a verified handshake. The latter four stay unknown, even on a consistent capture.

The inventory scans tracked and non-ignored untracked C-family source files across the checkout. It emits locations, lexical categories and a hash of its input, including generic raw reads/writes, dynamic mailbox offset names, SOC15 wrappers, indirect apertures, PCI configuration and BAR mappings. These are **candidates**, including comments and unrelated devices; manually trace them. This is not a C++ parser, complete call graph, binary audit, proof of reachability or evidence excluding writers. Ignored files, assembly, generated code, other kexts and firmware are outside its scope. The original P7.34 checks remain unchanged and detect changes to its reviewed sites and the literal disabled lease, but cannot prove absence of alternative writers.

## Reviewed paths and limits

* `RedNaviProbe::start` maps the register BAR through `IOPCIDevice`; `rd` and `wr` operate on that mapping using byte offsets. They accept computed offsets, so every generic candidate cannot be classified as MP1 without tracing its operands. The legacy `smuProbe` calls and `rn_smu13_send` calls in the later legacy path are after the Navi33 branch's return; they are potential writers in the source, not evidence of execution on Navi33. `smuProbe` requires `rednavi_smu=1`; `send` controls the legacy sends. `rn_smu13_send` polls, clears response, writes parameter and message, polls again and reads the result. Its mutex only coordinates cooperating RedNavi clients. Legacy timeout recovery writes a synthetic 1 to response, not firmware acknowledgment. The other legacy sender is in the graphics bring-up path; do not infer Navi33 reachability from the function alone.
* P7.27 `execute` uses a computed MP1 register plan and callbacks to `rd`/`wr`, with three dynamic write sites. Admission requires the unsafe build, opt-in, hardware and firmware prerequisites and mailbox lease. The lease remains hardcoded false; the shared RedNavi mutex does not provide external arbitration. P7.36/37 use bounded direct reads, and P7.37 reuses the P7.36 response read.
* SOC15 macros flow to `rn_rreg32`/`rn_wreg32`: direct BAR access when in range, otherwise RSMU index/data. A write to RSMU INDEX to select a firmware-flag read is not itself a C2PMSG write; RSMU DATA writes can target indirect registers and require operand analysis. MM_INDEX/DATA are VRAM access paths, not automatically MP1 writes. PCI configuration accesses are a different address space. This categorization does not rule out aliasing or unknown computed paths.
* IORegistry attachment/provider/client and `isOpen()` snapshots describe reported service relationships and open state at a moment, not the provenance of individual BAR transactions. An already-created mapping has its own lifetime; `isOpen()==false` cannot prove it has disappeared or prevent another mapping, a firmware writer, or a race. Apple documents [BAR mapping](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/kernel/iopcidevice/1811470-mapdevicememorywithregister) and [IOService open arbitration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/kernel/ioservice/1810174-handleopen); neither establishes a hardware-wide SMU mailbox lease. AMDSupport and boot framebuffer presence are observations, not proof that either wrote or did not write this mailbox.

## Linux comparison

For a fixed reference, Linux v6.12 uses the [SMU 13.0.7 power-play implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu13/smu_v13_0_7_ppt.c) and [common SMU transport](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu_cmn.c), with [SMU 13 lifecycle support](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu13/smu_v13_0.c). The common transport serializes cooperating Linux calls, waits for the previous response, clears response, writes parameter then message, waits for completion, decodes status and may read the result. Linux's ASIC-specific mappings, firmware readiness and lifecycle are prerequisites; transport sequence alone does not recreate Linux's ownership environment on macOS. The RedNavi mutex cannot serialize Apple or firmware. No inference from Navi32 behavior establishes Navi33 ownership or validates its command IDs and firmware state.

## Next evidence

A future design needs independently reviewed, enforceable coordination for all host access over the entire transaction, a defined firmware lifecycle and recovery procedure, and evidence that pre-existing mappings and other writers are accounted for. Any eventual live test needs a separately reviewed risk assessment, exact procedure and rollback path. The present passive capture can establish only sampled values and, when consistent, differences between endpoints; it cannot identify a writer or prove a fresh handshake. Native acceleration and Metal remain unverified.

## Offline source change review

`--compare-ref <commit-or-branch>` compares the current checkout's candidate multiset with tracked source archived from a Git commit. It reports added and removed candidates, including repeated identical lines; any added, removed or modified in-scope source file marks `review_required` and returns exit 2, including changes outside candidate lines. Moving an unchanged line does not create a candidate-line change, but still marks its file for review. The comparison checks full-line digests even when excerpts are truncated. The reference omits untracked files while the current scan includes non-ignored untracked files. Specify a fixed commit SHA for reproducibility. An unresolved reference fails closed. A zero delta is not a complete review: ignored or generated files, external code and non-C-family sources are outside the comparison, and the scanner cannot establish reachability or rule out writes.

Example: `python3 tools/navi33_p739_writer_provenance.py --repo . --compare-ref 234fb33589a1940962f6763c345823108c79a8ac --input /path/to/saved-ioreg.txt --output /path/to/new-comparison.json`

### Coordination gaps found in source

This section records the P7.39 baseline. P7.40 subsequently adds image-wide cooperative SMU serialization; see [P7.40](NAVI33_P740_HOST_MAILBOX_COORDINATOR.md) for its scope and remaining gaps.

`gpu.smu_msg_lock` belongs to each `RedNaviProbe` object's `rn_device`, initialized after its BAR mapping. The temporary legacy device explicitly points at **that instance's** lock; it does not make a device-wide lock across independent services. The image-wide atomic attempt latch prevents a second P7.27 attempt in that image, but does not serialize `rn_smu13_send`, raw `wr`, a different loaded driver, or firmware. `mmio_idx_lock` belongs to an `rn_device` too; it protects cooperating index/data operations on that object, not all aliases or a whole SMU transaction. Direct `rd`/`wr` do not automatically acquire either lock. A future cooperative implementation needs a single lifetime-managed coordination domain keyed to the physical device, coverage of every participating route (including indirect index/data and teardown), a defined lock order, and a way to establish participation and revocation over the complete transaction. Even that only coordinates participants: a separate enforceable platform mechanism or independently reviewed proof is required for external writers and firmware protocol/lifecycle. Current code has no such mechanism. Neither a shared software mutex nor an image-wide latch is an ownership lease.
